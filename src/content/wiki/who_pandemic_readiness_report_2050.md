The third annual WHO Pandemic Readiness Report (WPRR) was released on the 20th of January 2050 by the [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization) to assess the global readiness for emerging pandemics. The creation of reports every 5 years was a reaction to a series of severe local epidemics in the 2030s. The document details the current global political and economic situation and assesses the world’s readiness to respond to threats comparable to the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Report Content

### Global Economic Situation

In the last decade the trend of gradual deglobalization has slowly been reversed. With the improved economy after the period of economic instability in the twenties, the shift to the right ended and public perception once again changed in favor of globalization. This leaves the world in a unique situation, where many supply chain dependencies from the early 2000s no longer exist. At the same time the advantages of a globalized world, such as internationally connected knowledge networks or improved communication channels, can be reaped. The WHO judges this to be a decisive advantage, should a new pandemic arise.

### Key Actors

The WPRR identifies three actors with crucial functions and capabilities in economics, geopolitics, and pharmaceutical challenges, the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union), [United States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), and the [People’s Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China).

#### European Union

According to the WHO, the rise of global economic competition, combined with a decrease in international cooperation, led the member states of the European Union to further integrate, thus assigning the EU Commission advanced competencies, which are relevant to fight pandemics under a common approach and strategy. While the WPRR welcomes this advancement in partnership, it also warns that collaboration could be impaired by the return of globalization encouraging member states to pursue outside alliances and individual strategies rather than relying on the EU. However, despite its focus on new alliances, diplomatic interactions with Russia remain at a historical low.

#### United States of America

Over the last decades, US governments have repeatedly fundamentally shifted their international partnerships and pandemic prevention policies as part of their election promises. Notably, they have left the WHO twice, in 2026, 2038, only to rejoin 4 and 10 years later, respectively. The report argues that this has led to the US being an unreliable ally to other states, isolating it in global politics. Consequently, the WPRR criticizes a lack of foundation for the cooperation to fight off pandemics.

#### People’s Republic of China

Despite having maintained its autocratic state design, the WHO commends their technological advancement and pandemic prevention and defense capabilities. Over the last three decades, China has successfully caught up with economic and technological standards once established by Europe and the US.

#### Russia

Following the [Ukraine Peace Agreement](ukraine_peace_agreement), Russia remained isolated from Western states, with strongly reduced economic and political cooperation. Thus, the WPRR states that in the case of a new pandemic, Russia is not likely to partner with Western states on disease control or prevention.

### International Pandemic Preparedness

#### World Health Organization

As studies by the WHO show, the organization is still widely perceived as foundational to international disease control and general medical coordination. Nevertheless, the WPRR remarks that the WHO has repeatedly fallen into conflicts with member states such as the US, which may prove to reduce willingness to cooperate or accept WHO’s authority in the case of an emergency. Therefore, the report recommends for WHO to more strongly focus on its role of a mediating organization rather than independently pursue local initiatives.

Notwithstanding, the WPRR positively comments on The WHO’s well-prepared pandemic defense division, which is said to be able to quickly respond to new epidemics and form an Emergency Committee, if necessary.

#### Pandemic Treaty

As several pandemics have shown, the 2025 WHO Pandemic Agreement and PABS-annex still play a central role. It puts member countries of the WHO in a good position to detect and deal with dangerous diseases. While the PABS-annex ultimately settled on a hybrid model, as suggested by the EU, several instances have shown that companies are willing to work within the WHO’s system and agree to benefit sharing. Nonetheless, the WHO sees a risk that a more dangerous disease may lead to the opposite. The annex has also led to increased support of preexisting databases such as GenBank and Pathoplexus, to the advantage of the larger science community.
